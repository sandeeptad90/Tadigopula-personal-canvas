Techniques

These mirror the ideas you asked for: touch, rhythm, low stimulation, and gradually fading parent help.

🤲 Back tapping / pat-pat method

Most useful

Slow, steady pats near the shoulder blades or upper back can be calming. Keep the rhythm boring and predictable.

💆 Hair / head stroking

Very calming

Gentle forehead-to-crown strokes or smoothing the hair can help many children settle.

🫸 Chest / sternum press

Grounding cue

Slow, light hand pressure on the chest or upper body can feel reassuring. Use it as a brief settle cue, not as a long crutch.

🌬️ Fan / white noise

Low and safe

Keep airflow soft and point the fan away from the face. The goal is a calm background hum, not strong wind.

🗣️ Monotone story

Underrated

Use short, repetitive, boring phrases. The calmer the voice, the better the body can switch off.

🫁 Breathing sync

Advanced

Lie beside him and breathe slowly so his body copies your rhythm. Great for active or anxious children.

Best strategy: help him settle with touch, then slowly reduce the touch over time so sleep no longer depends on it.

Pregnancy

Safety first

Pregnancy with blood-thinner treatment and a spleen issue should stay clinician-guided. This section keeps the advice gentle and conservative.

Trimester Movement Guidance

High-risk pregnancies should prioritize consistency, circulation and fatigue management instead of aggressive fitness goals.

Short gentle walks and frequent movement breaks are usually safer than long intense sessions.

Daily Steps Guidance

Split movement into smaller sessions

T1

1st Trimester

3,000–4,000 steps daily split across two short gentle walks. Rest is equally important during early pregnancy.

Gentle only
T2

2nd Trimester

Usually the safest trimester for light exercise if approved by OB/MFM. Swimming and prenatal mobility may be added.

Most active phase
T3

3rd Trimester

Scale movement back again as balance changes and fatigue increases. Focus on circulation and comfort.

Short walks only

🤰 Mom’s pregnancy basics

  • Prenatal vitamin daily.
  • Iron tablet as prescribed.
  • Hydration throughout the day.
  • Walking, yoga, pelvic floor work, and swimming if cleared.

🚫 Keep it conservative

  • No contact sports or injury-risk activities.
  • No “detox” cleanses that replace meals.
  • No medicine changes without clinician approval.
  • Stop and ask the OB team about bleeding, pain, dizziness, or unusual symptoms.

Kidney stone history: keep UNDER 100g, food only - no powders

Kidney Stone Rule for Protein

Get protein from food only - no whey or protein powders.

Animal protein max: 1 serving per day (about 3-4 oz) unless her doctor says otherwise.

Drink water with every single meal and snack to keep urine diluted.

Add lemon or lime to water daily because citrate helps prevent stones.

Food-first strategy: protein from yogurt, eggs, dal, paneer, tofu, lentils, chickpeas, fish, chicken, goat and milk.

Good Protein Foods to Rotate

Greek yogurt, eggs, moong dal chilla, lentils, chole, paneer, tofu, chickpeas, milk, chicken, fish, goat, hummus, pumpkin seeds and cottage cheese.

Use smaller portions through the day instead of one very large protein-heavy meal.

Pregnancy Supplements - Discuss With OB

- Prenatal Vitamin with Folate (methylfolate preferred, not just folic acid)

- Vitamin D3 - Indian-origin women in Virginia are commonly deficient

- DHA/Omega-3 for baby brain development (Nordic Naturals at Costco VA)

- Iron supplement only if blood work shows anemia

- Calcium from food preferred over supplements with kidney stone history

- NO Vitamin C supplements - increases oxalate and kidney stone risk

Family Food Rhythm

Keep breakfast protein-rich, lunch balanced, evening snack light but useful, and dinner early enough that sleep is not delayed.

Use vegetables like kale, broccoli, cauliflower, bok choy, carrots and green beans more often than spinach when kidney-stone prevention matters.

Most Important Rule - Hydration

Drink at least 3 liters (100 oz) of water daily.

This is the single most effective way to prevent kidney stones.

It also helps with APS clot prevention and pregnancy overall.

Urine should always be pale yellow - never dark yellow.

Add fresh squeezed lemon or lime to water every morning.

Safety

Virginia Doctors to See Immediately

- Hematologist (existing) - inform about pregnancy, discuss Lovenox

- OB/GYN - keep informed about all conditions

- Maternal-Fetal Medicine specialist (Inova / VCU / UVA)

- Urologist - kidney stone history plus pregnancy needs monitoring

Splenomegaly in Pregnancy

Extra caution as pregnancy progresses

1

No contact or impact activities ever

Spleen risk remains throughout all trimesters.

2

No heavy lifting

Avoid anything over 10 lbs and discuss practical lifting with OB if needed.

3

Stop if upper left abdominal pain

Could indicate spleen stress. Call doctor immediately, do not wait.

4

Ask OB to monitor spleen in ultrasounds

Growing uterus shifts organs. Spleen size should be checked.

APS in Pregnancy - Critical Points

High-risk pregnancy protocol - non-negotiable

1

Lovenox injections plus aspirin is standard APS protocol

Call hematologist this week to confirm if Lovenox has been started.

2

Legs Up Wall pose every single evening

A simple clot-prevention routine that also helps swelling.

3

Never sit still for over 1 hour

Get up, do ankle pumps, walk to kitchen. Keep blood moving.

4

Wear compression socks from 2nd trimester

Especially on long trips or long sitting days. Ask OB for compression grade.

5

Ultrasounds every 2-4 weeks

APS can affect placenta blood flow. MFM monitors this closely.

6

Watch for DVT signs

Calf pain, redness, one leg swollen more than the other - go to ER immediately.
