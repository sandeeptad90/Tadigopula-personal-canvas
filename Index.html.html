Touch Techniques

These mirror the ideas from your JSX: rhythmic touch, soft pressure, low-stimulation sound, and gradual fading.

🤲 Back tapping / pat-pat method

Most effective

Use a slow, steady rhythm near the shoulder blades. The goal is to calm him, then slowly taper the taps until he no longer needs them.

💆 Hair / head stroking

Extremely powerful

Gentle forehead-to-crown strokes or eyebrow sweeps. Keep pressure feather-light and slow.

🫸 Chest / sternum press

Great for active kids

Slow, gentle palm pressure on the chest area can feel grounding and safe. Best as a brief settling cue, not a constant crutch.

🌬️ Fan / white noise

Non-negotiable buy

Use a low, safe level. Point airflow toward the wall or ceiling, not directly at him.

🗣️ Monotone story

Underrated

A flat, slow, boring voice reduces excitement. Think “quiet clouds, soft grass, gentle wind.”

🫁 Breathing sync

Advanced

Lie beside him and breathe slowly so his body can match yours. Use it as a calm-down cue, not a performance.

🦶 Foot / leg massage

Starter ritual

Nice before books or as a transition into the bedroom. Make the ritual name consistent so the brain learns the pattern.

Best strategy from your file: use touch to help him settle, then fade it gradually so the body learns “I can fall asleep now.”

Activities That Help Sleep

Swimming — best overall sleep activity

Your file ranks it highest because it uses the whole body and is ideal on weekend mornings.

  • Best time: 9:00–10:00 AM on Saturday.
  • Avoid late evening swims.

Taekwondo / martial arts

Good for discipline, focus, and movement. Works well after school or on Saturday morning.

Painting / art class

Great for creativity and fine motor skills, but it is not a strong sleep-tiring activity by itself.

Soccer / sports class

Good running-heavy activity. Great if you can get it in earlier in the day.
